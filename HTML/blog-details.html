How to choose noise-cancelling headphones

What the numbers on the box mean, and which ones you can ignore.

Noise cancelling has gone from a premium extra to a standard feature. That makes choosing harder, because every box now promises the same thing. Here is how to read the specs and what to test in the store.

Start with where you will wear them

Commuters need strong low-frequency cancelling for engines and trains. Office workers benefit more from cancelling in the mid range, where voices sit. Adaptive modes switch between the two automatically.

Battery claims are usually measured with noise cancelling off. Look for the number with it on.

The three specs that matter

  • Battery with ANC on: 25 hours or more covers a long-haul flight.
  • Weight: under 260 g is comfortable for all-day wear.
  • Multipoint: connects to your phone and laptop at the same time.

What you can ignore

Frequency response ranges beyond 20 kHz and driver sizes above 40 mm rarely change what you hear. Comfort and fit matter far more.
